## 5. LinkedIn Post Bank (ready to schedule)

**Post 1 — The core hook (POV):**
Your experience is in the top 5% of your industry.
Your online presence isn't in the top 50%.

That gap is the only thing standing between you and the next board seat, the next speaking invite, the next inbound consulting deal.

Visibility isn't vanity for senior professionals. It's infrastructure.

**Post 2 — The Google search hook:**
Quick exercise: search your own name on Google right now.

What comes up on page one?

If it's a decade-old conference bio, a LinkedIn profile you haven't touched since 2022, or nothing at all — that's the gap we close.

**Post 3 — Reframe (story format):**
A CHRO we spoke to had led three major transformations, sat on two advisory boards, and mentored a dozen HR leaders who now run their own functions.

Her Google search results? A single outdated bio from a conference in 2019.

Results and visibility are two different jobs. Most senior leaders only have time for one.

**Post 4 — Contrarian/POV:**
Nobody tells senior HR leaders this: doing great work quietly is a career ceiling, not a virtue.

The leaders getting board seats and keynote invites aren't necessarily the best at the job. They're the most visible ones who are also good at the job.

**Post 5 — Listicle:**
3 signs your personal brand hasn't caught up to your career:

1. Recruiters find your LinkedIn, not the other way around.
2. Your name search shows nothing you'd want a board member to see.
3. You've never been asked to speak, write, or advise — despite being qualified for all three.

**Post 6 — Educational (thought leadership, blog-adjacent):**
Why executives need personal branding — and why "I'm too senior for that" is exactly backwards:

The more senior you are, the more your next opportunity depends on people who've never worked with you directly. A recruiter. A board nominating committee. A journalist. They only have what's visible to go on.

**Post 7 — CTA-forward:**
We work with a small number of HR leaders and consultants at a time — building the website, Google presence, and LinkedIn strategy that should already exist given what they've achieved.

If your results have outgrown your visibility, that's a conversation worth having.

**Post 8 — Aspirational close:**
The goal isn't more followers.

It's the day a board seat, a keynote invite, or a six-figure consulting engagement lands in your inbox — because someone searched your name and found exactly what they were hoping for.

That's what "being prominent" is actually for.
